How it is worked out

Volumetric weight (kg) = length × width × height (cm) ÷ divisor

The courier compares that figure with what the parcel actually weighs and bills you on the higher of the two. That higher figure is the chargeable weight.

Which divisor?

DivisorWhere you'll usually see it
5000Common for international express services
6000The IATA standard for air cargo, and used by some economy services
4000Some services that price space more heavily

The divisor is set by the courier and the service, so check your rate card. A smaller divisor gives a higher volumetric weight.

A worked example

A comforter packed in a 60 × 40 × 40 cm box weighs 8 kg. Its volume is 96,000 cm³, so with a divisor of 5000 its volumetric weight is 19.2 kg. The courier bills 19.2 kg, which means 11.2 kg of the charge is for air. Vacuum-pack the same comforter into a 40 × 30 × 20 cm box and the volumetric weight drops to 4.8 kg, so it is billed on its actual 8 kg.

Questions

What is volumetric weight?
Volumetric weight, also called dimensional weight, is a weight worked out from a parcel's size rather than its mass. Couriers use it because a large, light parcel takes up as much space on a van or plane as a heavy one.
How do I calculate volumetric weight in kg?
Multiply length, width and height in centimetres and divide by the courier's divisor. A 60 × 40 × 40 cm box with a divisor of 5000 has a volumetric weight of 96,000 ÷ 5000 = 19.2 kg.
What is chargeable weight?
Chargeable weight is the weight you are billed on: the higher of the actual weight and the volumetric weight.
Which divisor should I use?
Use the divisor on your courier's rate card. 5000 is common for international express services and 6000 is the IATA standard for air cargo; some services use other values, such as 4000.
How can I reduce volumetric weight?
Ship in the smallest box the product fits, avoid empty space and filler, and compress soft goods such as pillows, comforters and towels, for example by vacuum packing.
